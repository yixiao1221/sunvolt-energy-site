How to Sell Portable Power Stations: B2B and D2C Pricing Playbook for Africa & Middle East

August 27, 2026 · 9 min read · By SunVolt Energy

Portable power stations are no longer a niche camping accessory. In Africa and the Middle East, they have become a practical solution for load shedding, unstable grids, small businesses, and off-grid homes. That creates a real commercial opportunity for importers who understand how to price for both wholesale buyers and end consumers.

This playbook explains the two main sales models: B2B wholesale and direct-to-consumer. It covers margin structure, landed cost, bundling, shipping, and payment methods so you can build a product line with a clear profit path.

Start with Your Landed Cost

Your pricing should start from landed cost, not the factory EXW price. Landed cost includes the product, export packaging, sea or air freight, insurance, import duty, customs brokerage, and inland delivery. A simple planning formula is:

Landed cost = FOB price + freight + insurance + import duty + local clearance + final delivery

For a 500Wh portable power station with an FOB price around $140, a typical importer may add $12-$18 for sea freight and port handling per unit, plus duty and local logistics. The total may land at $165-$190 before marketing and after-sales costs.

B2B Wholesale Pricing

Wholesale buyers usually purchase by the container or pallet. They expect consistent pricing, stable supply, and enough margin to distribute further. A common B2B structure is:

  • Wholesale price — landed cost plus 25-40% gross margin
  • Distributor price — wholesale price plus 15-25% for regional distributors
  • Retail reference price — distributor price plus 30-50% for shops and online sellers

For B2B customers, avoid competing on price alone. Offer value through sample support, consistent packaging, certification documents, replacement parts, and a clear warranty. Buyers in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, UAE, Saudi Arabia, and other markets are willing to pay for a supplier who reduces their risk.

D2C and Retail Pricing

Direct-to-consumer pricing should be higher because it includes platform fees, payment processing, customer support, returns, and one-piece shipping. A D2C retail price is commonly 2.2-3.0 times the landed cost for a small power station.

If you sell through a marketplace, add 10-20% for platform commission and another 5-10% for payment fees. If you sell through WhatsApp, Instagram, or your own website, you can keep more margin but should still build support and delivery into the price.

Sell Kits, Not Just Units

The highest-margin strategy is to bundle a power station with a solar panel, carry bag, and local plug adapters. A customer who buys a unit alone may not return soon. A customer who buys a complete solar backup kit becomes a stronger source of repeat sales for accessories and larger units.

Suggested entry bundles:

  • Shop starter kit — 500Wh station, 100W solar panel, LED light, universal plugs
  • Clinic backup kit — 500Wh or 1000Wh station, two 100W panels, extension cable
  • Outdoor vendor kit — 300Wh station, foldable panel, portable light and phone charging cable

How to Structure B2B Offers

For importers and distributors, provide clear terms from the first conversation:

  • Pricing terms — EXW, FOB, CIF, or DDP depending on the buyer's experience
  • MOQ — standard models can start from 10 units; OEM usually requires more
  • Lead time — sample orders and production schedules clearly stated
  • Warranty — battery and product coverage, plus replacement policy
  • Documentation — CE, FCC, RoHS, UN38.3, and MSDS as required

Payment Methods for International Buyers

Your payment mix should match how your buyers prefer to transact. For B2B buyers, T/T bank transfer is still common, often with a deposit and balance before shipment. Trade platforms such as Alibaba Trade Assurance can add buyer confidence for first orders.

For D2C sales, cards and digital wallets are the most convenient. PayPal and Stripe work well in many markets, while USDT is increasingly used by buyers who want fast cross-border settlement without high bank fees. Whatever method you choose, make the payment flow clear on your product page and provide a confirmation message after purchase.

Build a Repeatable Sales Funnel

  1. Attract — use search content, WhatsApp status, and short product videos.
  2. Educate — send a simple comparison sheet and answer use-case questions.
  3. Offer — quote wholesale and D2C prices separately.
  4. Close — confirm payment terms, shipping, and delivery window.
  5. Follow up — ask for a photo or review and offer the next model or accessory.

For importers, the best content is practical: show the unit running a fan, freezer, router, or clinic equipment. A short video showing actual usage is far more persuasive than a long specification list.

Start with a Focused Product Range

Do not launch with every capacity and every color. Start with three models that cover your market: a low-cost entry model, a mid-range volume seller, and a higher-capacity premium option. This makes inventory easier, improves product knowledge, and gives customers a clear upgrade path.
