South Africa: if load shedding has eased, why are businesses still buying storage?

October 18, 2026 · 5 min read · By SunVolt Energy

Load shedding has eased in South Africa, and that has changed the sales conversation for storage. A battery sold purely as a load-shedding device now has to justify itself on different numbers — and for many businesses it still does.

What changed

Reporting in 2026 described how South Africa reduced load shedding without adding generation capacity, and the conversation moved on: storage is being sold for peak tariffs, solar self-consumption and general resilience rather than only for scheduled outages. As one October 2026 article put it, your battery was never only for load shedding.

Four reasons storage still pays

1. Time-of-use and peak tariffs

Where the tariff changes by time of day, a battery charged off-peak and discharged at peak shifts the same kWh into a cheaper window. That is a daily, predictable saving that does not depend on an outage.

2. Solar self-consumption

Without storage, solar exported to the grid earns an export rate that is usually lower than the retail rate. A battery keeps the daytime generation on site.

3. Outages that are not load shedding

Equipment failure, cable theft and local network faults still interrupt supply. The loads that matter — refrigeration, IT, POS, security — need to ride through those regardless of the national supply picture.

4. Diesel avoidance

Where a generator is installed as backup, a battery can cover short outages so the generator does not start. Fewer starts, less fuel, less noise.

How the sizing target changes

A load-shedding system is sized to cover a two to four hour block. A tariff-shifting system is sized on daily energy: how many kWh move from peak to off-peak, and how many cycles the battery will do each day. Those are different numbers, and the second usually needs more usable capacity but less peak power.

What buyers should ask

  • Usable kWh after depth of discharge, not nameplate capacity.
  • Cycle life at the depth of discharge the warranty actually covers.
  • Whether the inverter can arbitrage on a schedule, not only provide backup.
  • What the warranty says about daily cycling.
