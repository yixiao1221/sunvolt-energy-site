Step 1: Understand the Certifications You Need

Before ordering, confirm which certificates your target market requires. Most buyers need at least the following:

  • CE — required for the European market
  • FCC — required for the United States
  • RoHS — restricted substances compliance
  • UN38.3 — lithium battery transport safety test (mandatory for air and sea freight)
  • MSDS — material safety data sheet required by carriers

Ask the factory for the test reports up front. A serious manufacturer will provide certificates together with the quotation, not after the deposit is paid.

Step 2: Evaluate the Battery and BMS Quality

The battery cell is the most expensive component and the main source of warranty issues. Insist on Grade A LiFePO4 cells, not recycled or B-grade cells that degrade quickly. Check the claimed cycle life (reputable LiFePO4 products offer 3,000+ cycles to 80% capacity) and confirm the unit has a proper BMS with over-charge, over-discharge, and temperature protection. Step 4: Plan Lithium Battery Shipping

Power stations contain lithium-ion batteries, classified as Class 9 dangerous goods. This affects both air and sea freight:

  • Sea freight is the standard for volume orders and the most economical
  • Air freight is fast but expensive and strictly limited by watt-hour rating and quantity
  • DDP terms are available for many markets if you prefer the factory to handle clearance

Work with a forwarder experienced in lithium battery cargo, and make sure the factory provides UN38.3 and MSDS documentation to avoid port delays.

Step 5: Clear Customs Correctly

Portable power stations are typically classified under HS code 8507.60 (lithium-ion accumulators), but classification can vary by country and product function. Confirm the exact code with your customs broker before shipment. Some African markets require additional conformity programs such as SONCAP for Nigeria; your supplier should be able to support these applications.

Step 6: Start with Samples, Then Scale

The safest path for first-time importers is to order 2-3 sample units, test them in your market, and then commit to a container order. This validates product quality, packaging, and customer demand before you invest in volume inventory.
