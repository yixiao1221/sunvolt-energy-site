Sizing Method

How We Size an Integrated System

The design starts from the load, not from a product list. Four numbers decide the equipment:

  1. Daily energy (kWh/day). Add every load with its wattage and daily hours. Separate daytime load from night load.
  2. Peak load (kW). Add the appliances that can run at the same time, then add surge for motors and compressors.
  3. PV array (kWp). Daily energy divided by local peak-sun hours, plus losses and battery charging headroom.
  4. Battery capacity (kWh). Night load plus a reserve, divided by usable depth of discharge.

Worked example: a villa using 20 kWh per day, with 6 kW peak load and 5 peak-sun hours, needs roughly 5-6 kWp of PV, a 6-8 kW hybrid inverter and a battery sized for the night block plus one day of autonomy. The exact figures are confirmed after the load list is reviewed.

Procurement Checklist

What to Ask Before You Buy an Integrated System

  • Usable battery capacity after depth of discharge, not the nameplate figure.
  • Cycle life at the depth of discharge the warranty actually covers.
  • Inverter efficiency and surge rating for motor loads.
  • Which certifications and test reports are available for the battery, inverter and panels.
  • Warranty length by component, and who handles the claim in the destination country.
  • Spare parts: BMS boards, fans, fuses and communication modules.
  • Monitoring platform, data plan and remote support access.
  • Lead time for the full kit, plus packing and container loading plan.
